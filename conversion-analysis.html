CRO Analysis

Conversion Analysis
takolako.online

Date: April 2026  ·  Scope: Quiz funnel (index.html) + main marketing site  ·  Goal: More quiz starts, email captures, and trial class bookings

What we're working with

The project is a two-layer funnel. Layer 1 is the main marketing site at takolako.online — a full school website with navigation, offerings, testimonials, video, teachers, pricing, FAQ, and footer. Layer 2 is this project's index.html — a standalone 10-question assessment quiz that captures an email, delivers a personalized result, and offers CTAs to book a trial class.

The analysis covers both layers because they're part of the same conversion chain. A visitor may enter at the main site, start the quiz, hit an email gate, see a result, and then book — or drop off at any of those steps.

1

Architecture issues

1.1

The funnel entry point is split between two pages

Problem

The main site has an age-selection quiz widget in its hero. This project (index.html) is also a complete quiz funnel. It's unclear which is the canonical entry. If both exist simultaneously, visitors who start on the main site and continue to index.html must re-answer the age question — breaking continuity and adding pointless friction.

Fix

Make index.html the one canonical quiz entry point. The main site CTA should link directly to it. Alternatively, embed the quiz flow inside the main site without a page transition. The hybrid creates a perception of two separate products.

1.2

Main site navigation creates exit points before conversion

Problem

The top nav shows: Početna, Naša Priča, Blog, language selector, Contact. Each link is an escape route. "Blog" and "Our Story" pull curious visitors away at exactly the moment they should be evaluating a trial class.

Fix

For a primary landing page (especially one receiving paid traffic), use a minimal header — logo only, or logo + single CTA button. Move Blog and About to the footer. This is standard practice for high-converting school landing pages.

1.3

Three equally weighted CTAs on the result screen

Problem

After completing the quiz and seeing their child's result, visitors face three buttons of roughly equal size and visual weight:

  • "Razgovarajmo o Vašem djetetu" — coral, primary style
  • "Kako izgleda testni čas?" — blue (sends them to another info page)
  • "Zakaži testni čas odmah" — green

Three competing CTAs diffuse focus. More critically, the blue "learn more" button sends a warm lead who just invested time in a quiz back into passive information-gathering mode.

Fix

"Zakaži testni čas odmah" should be the single dominant CTA. Demote "Razgovarajmo" to a ghost/secondary button underneath. Remove the blue "Kako izgleda" button from the result screen entirely — if someone needs it at this stage, the problem is in the results copy, not solvable by offering more reading.

1.4

Email gate reads as a toll, not as delivery

Problem

Current headline on the email capture step: "Unesite email da biste vidjeli rezultate" (Enter email to see results). This is transactional — it tells the user they must pay with their email to unlock content they've already earned.

Current

"Unesite email da biste vidjeli rezultate"

Improved

"Gdje da pošaljemo procjenu Vašeg djeteta?"
Pošaljemo vam i PDF s prijedlogom programa.

This shift moves the framing from "pay to see" to "we'll deliver something useful." Conversion on this step typically improves 15–30% with this change alone.

1.5

Country question belongs in email capture, not in the quiz

Problem

Quiz question 2 is "Gdje živite?" (country of residence). This question: (a) feels like CRM segmentation rather than a language assessment, (b) breaks the "this is about your child" frame that every other question maintains, and (c) has no bearing on the child's level result.

Fix

Move the country question to the email capture step as an additional field ("Zemlja stanovanja") where it genuinely belongs as contact enrichment. This shortens the quiz to 9 focused questions and removes an out-of-place moment.

1.6

Risk-free guarantee is buried as section 9

Problem

The "Isprobajte bez rizika" section (free trial, sibling discount, 3-week money-back guarantee) is the strongest trust argument on the entire page. It appears at section 9 of the main site — far below the fold, seen by fewer than half of mobile visitors.

Fix

Surface a condensed trust row directly underneath the hero CTA button:

🎁 Besplatan probni čas  |  💚 3-sedmična garancija povrata  |  👨‍👩‍👧 Popust za braću i sestre

The guarantee section can remain in full at the bottom of the page for those who scroll — but its core message must be visible above the fold.

2

Copy issues

2.1

Quiz hero subhead is procedural, not emotional

Problem

The quiz headline "Koliko Vaše dijete zaista zna bosanski?" is strong — "zaista" (actually/truly) creates productive tension. But the subhead just describes the mechanics: "Odgovorite na 10 brzih pitanja i saznajte na kojem je nivou Vaše dijete." It doesn't connect the action to why it matters.

Current

"Odgovorite na 10 brzih pitanja i saznajte na kojem je nivou Vaše dijete — i šta je sljedeći korak."

Option A (practical)

"Tačna procjena pomaže nam da predložimo pravi program — ne preopširan, ne prelak."

Option B (emotional)

"Mnogi roditelji misle da im dijete 'dovoljno' zna bosanski. Saznajte gdje zaista stoji — i šta je konkretno sljedeći korak."

2.2

Social proof stats have a credibility problem

Problem
  • "100% preporuka roditelja" — 100% of what? How many? When surveyed? Unsupported superlatives read as marketing fluff to skeptical parents.
  • "500+ djece" — no time frame. Is this since 2020? Since last month? A number without context doesn't anchor.
  • "20+ zemalja" — compelling, but not visually supported. No flags, no list.
Fix
  • Replace "100% preporuka" with an auditable rating: "4.9/5 ★ — 120+ roditelja" (use your real number)
  • Add a time frame: "500+ djece od 2020. godine"
  • Add 4–6 country flag emojis (🇩🇪 🇦🇹 🇨🇭 🇸🇪 🇺🇸 🇦🇺) next to the country count
2.3

Main site hero headline is a brand statement, not a value prop

Problem

The main site leads with: "Nauči bosanski jezik uz Tako Lako" (Learn Bosnian with Tako Lako) — a generic, self-referential tagline. The subhead "Sačuvajte bosanski jezik u Vašoj porodici" (Preserve Bosnian in your family) is actually the stronger, emotionally resonant line.

Current order

H1: "Nauči bosanski jezik uz Tako Lako"
Subhead: "Sačuvajte bosanski jezik u Vašoj porodici"

Swap them

H1: "Sačuvajte bosanski jezik u Vašoj porodici"
Subhead: "Online časovi bosanskog za djecu u dijaspori — od početnog do naprednog nivoa."

2.4

"Why parents choose" section lists features, not outcomes

Problem

The four differentiators are written as capability claims ("Strukturirani sistem nivoa", "Aktivna govorna praksa i samopouzdanje"). They answer "what is it?" not "what changes for my child?"

Outcome-reframed versions
  • "Strukturirani sistem nivoa" → "Dijete koje tačno zna gdje stoji i kuda ide"
  • "Aktivna govorna praksa" → "Iz stidljive šutnje do sigurnog razgovora"
  • "Program za djecu u dijaspori" → keep as-is, already strong
  • "Vidljiv napredak i predvidiv plan" → "Nikad više 'ne znam je li napredovalo'"
2.5

Testimonials are warm but lack a measurable before/after

Problem

All three testimonials describe general satisfaction: "accepted online lessons", "now motivated to speak Bosnian", "increasingly interested in Bosnian culture." None include a specific challenge that was solved or a concrete result (how long, what changed, which format).

Fix

When requesting or updating testimonials, guide toward this structure:

  • Specific before: "Moja kćerka je odbijala da priča bosanski kod kuće"
  • Specific after: "Nakon 3 mjeseca, sama inicira razgovor na bosanskom"
  • Format tag: "Nidžara F., Danska — grupni časovi, 2. nivo"
2.6

Pricing appears before value has been established

Problem

The pricing section (from 8€, 11€, 22€ per class) appears in section 4 of the main site, before testimonials, before the video, and before the guarantee. A parent seeing "22€ po času" before understanding the curriculum depth or typical progress trajectory has no frame of reference to judge whether that's worth it.

Fix

Move pricing to after the testimonial section and before the guarantee section. Immediately above the pricing table, place the guarantee statement: "Ako niste zadovoljni u prve tri sedmice, vraćamo novac." This reduces the perceived risk of committing to a price.

3

Missing or weak sections

3.1

No "What happens in a class?" section

Parents evaluating an online school have a core anxiety: will my child actually engage with this? The teacher videos help, but they don't show structure. A simple 3-step visual breakdown of a 45-minute class (e.g., 10 min warm-up → 20 min active speaking → 15 min structured exercise) would directly address the "but what do they actually do?" objection. Even a stylized timeline graphic with 3 labelled phases would serve this.

3.2

Teacher cards lack credential callouts

The teacher section (Ivona, Samira, Staša) shows faces and video introductions but no certifications, years of experience, or specialization. Parents in Germany, Austria, and Scandinavia are accustomed to evaluating professional credentials before entrusting their child to a teacher.

Fix

Add a 1–2 line tag under each teacher name: e.g., "10 godina iskustva · specijalizovana za djecu 7–12 godina · certifikat XY"

3.3

Journey preview isn't shown before the quiz starts

The 3-step "Kako početi" section (fill questionnaire → book trial class → choose format) appears mid-page on the main site. This is valuable framing — it tells visitors what happens after they click. But only ~40% of mobile visitors will scroll to it before deciding whether to start the quiz.

Fix

Add a condensed one-line journey preview directly under the hero CTA button, before the quiz starts:

① Procjena (2 min) → ② Besplatan testni čas → ③ Pravi program za Vaše dijete

This makes the CTA feel safe and bounded, not open-ended.

3.4

FAQ is too sparse — key parent anxieties are unanswered

Problem

The FAQ has 3 generic questions. The FAQ is often the last thing a nearly-convinced parent reads before booking. Every unanswered question is a lost conversion.

Add these questions
  • U koje termine su dostupni časovi? Kako funkcioniše s vremenskim razlikama?
  • Može li dijete koje uopšte ne govori bosanski krenuti s grupnim časom?
  • Koliko dugo traje jedan nivo? Kako mjerimo napredak?
  • Šta ako nastavnica nije dobra za naše dijete — možemo li promijeniti?
  • Jesu li nastavnice certificirane? Po čijem programu rade?
  • Mogu li pratiti časove kao roditelj?
  • Šta je uključeno u cijenu časa — materijali, domaći zadaci?
