## 1. THE PRODUCT

**What it does:** Online Bosnian language instruction for diaspora children (ages 4+). Individual, group, and family lessons with certified teachers and structured curriculum.

**Current category:** Online language school

**Should-be category:** "Structured Bosnian language program for diaspora families" — narrows to unique niche, no competition in that frame

---

## 2. FAILURE MODE DIAGNOSIS

- [x] **Too Generic** — "Nauči bosanski jezik uz Tako Lako" is a brand-first headline with no ICP signal
- [x] **Too Outcome-Only** — "Sačuvajte bosanski jezik u Vašoj porodici" states the outcome but not the mechanism or problem
- [ ] Too Horizontal — actually well-focused on diaspora kids
- [ ] Too Abstract
- [ ] Too Feature-Heavy
- [ ] Too Clever

**Primary failure mode:** Too Generic (hero) + Too Outcome-Only (subhead)
**Evidence:** Current headline centers the brand name, not the parent's problem. "Sačuvajte" is a command to action, not a recognition of the pain they already feel.

---

## 3. THE ICP

**As client describes it:** Children aged 4+ living abroad, multilingual households

**Actual ICP (narrowed):**
> Bosnian/Herzegovinian parents aged 28–45, living in Germany, Austria, or Sweden for 3–12 years, with children aged 5–12 who understand basic Bosnian but speak very little — especially with grandparents or during visits to Bosnia.

**The 3am problem:**
> We visited Bosnia last summer. My son couldn't hold a conversation with his dedo. My mother cried. We speak Bosnian at home but it's not enough. I've tried apps but they drop off after two weeks. I don't know what the right level is for him or where to start.

**Current alternative:** Language apps (Duolingo etc.), informal home practice, or nothing

---

## 4. THE PROBLEM

**Primary problem:** Diaspora children passively understand Bosnian but can't speak it — and the gap grows with every year. Parents feel guilty but don't know where to start or if the child is even at the right level.

**Consequence:** By age 12, the window for natural heritage language acquisition is narrowing. The child becomes permanently disconnected from family roots.

**Emotional weight:** Family identity, guilt, cultural belonging — very high emotional weight

**Does current copy name this?** ⚠️ Partially — quiz headline "Koliko Vaše dijete zaista zna bosanski?" creates tension but the main site hero doesn't name the underlying pain.

---

## 5. THE MECHANISM

**How it works:**
1. Parent takes a 10-question assessment for their child → level diagnosis
2. Matched to a certified teacher + appropriate group/individual format
3. Structured curriculum with measurable progress at each level

**Does current copy explain mechanism?** ✅ Yes — quiz funnel explains it well. Main site less so.

---

## 6. CATEGORY & DIFFERENTIATION

**Category strategy:** C — Create new category
"Online language school" is too generic. TakoLako is the only structured Bosnian program designed specifically for diaspora children — that's the category.

**Named alternative:** "Language apps that give up after week 2" / "Unstructured home practice that doesn't produce results"

**Sharpest differentiator (draft):**
> "The only structured Bosnian curriculum built for diaspora children — not adapted from textbooks, not an app. Real teachers, measurable levels."

**Sharpness test:** ✅ No competitor says exactly this in this market

## 10. POSITIONING DECK (6 SLIDES)

**Slide 1 — What:** The only structured Bosnian language program built for diaspora children — not adapted, not an app.

**Slide 2 — Who:** Diaspora parents (DE/AT/SE) with children 5–12 who understand Bosnian but can't speak it.

**Slide 3 — Problem:** Every year without structured learning, the language window narrows. Apps drop off. Home practice isn't enough. The gap grows.

**Slide 4 — How:** 10-question assessment → right level match → certified teacher → structured levels with measurable progress.

**Slide 5 — Why TakoLako:** Unlike apps, real teachers. Unlike generic schools, curriculum built for diaspora — not translated from textbooks.

**Slide 6 — Proof:** Parents report their children holding first real conversations with grandparents after 3 months.
